# When does "structure protects computation" survive scrutiny? A ledger across twenty tests

**tritsystem** (independent researcher)
Companion to `PREPRINT.md`. Draws on results from `topological-phononics/`, the Spikeling neuromorphic DSL bridge, the acoustic-vortex quasicrystal reservoir, the acoustic levitator control project, and one real-world-motivated network in `horde-beta-version-1` (jet-engine spike pipeline, 2026-08-09).

---

## Scope note — what this is NOT claiming

An earlier attempt at this kind of synthesis was made and retracted (`cross-substrate-synthesis-finding`, 2026-07-10): the pitch was that one "symmetry-conditional protection" law governs phononic, ternary, and acoustic substrates alike. It was measured, not assumed, and it lost — the acoustic mechanism (symmetry gating a nonlinearity directly) turned out to be a genuinely different phenomenon from the phononic/ternary one (symmetry gating a topological invariant one step removed from computation), and didn't even reproduce across its own substrate class (FEM plate → resonator bank, null).

This document does not repeat that claim. It is not a proposal that one mechanism unifies these results — the substrates below span tight-binding lattices, Duffing-nonlinear mechanical oscillators, magnetic exchange coupling, quasi-periodic tilings, and a staged spiking-neuron pipeline with no lattice structure at all; there is no single physical law connecting them. What is being reported is narrower and, we think, more useful: a **track record**. Across twenty independently pre-registered or independently-designed tests of the same hypothesis *shape* — "an exotic or reinforcing structural feature will make a reservoir/network more computationally robust or capable than the naive baseline" — how often did that hypothesis survive confound-controlled measurement? This is a base-rate finding for calibrating priors on the *next* instance of the hypothesis, not a claim about *why*.

## Method

Each row below is a distinct, real test run in this body of work, with its own pre-registration or explicit hypothesis, its own confound-hunting pass, and its own honest verdict already on record in the source repo or vault entry cited. Nothing here is re-derived from memory of the result — each row was read from the actual source file before being tabulated. Verdicts use four categories: **CONFIRMED** (survived a pre-registered or confound-controlled test), **INCONCLUSIVE** (leans a direction but the confidence interval spans the null), **NULL/REFUTED/REVERSED** (the hypothesis failed, or the effect ran opposite the prediction), **RETRACTED** (an earlier positive read was overturned by a sharper follow-up test on the same claim).

## Tally

Of 23 rows: **3 confirmed** (#1, #2, #11 — all narrow and conditional even where positive: chiral-symmetry-gated, within-architecture only, degrading under nonlinearity/noise), **2 inconclusive** (#6, #10 — both lean positive but fail their own pre-registered bar), **18 null, refuted, reversed, or retracted**. Two of the confirmed cases (#1, #11) and both inconclusive cases (#6, #10) concern the *same* underlying object — SSH chiral-symmetric defect protection — tested on different substrates (idealized lattice, Duffing mechanical model, real Spikeling LIF chain, real Spikeling Resonator). So out of roughly a dozen *distinct* hypotheses tested (collapsing repeated tests of the same SSH claim into one), effectively **one** survives cleanly: chiral-symmetry-conditional topological defect-tolerance, and even that one does not transfer at full idealized magnitude to a real device (#5, #6) or to a differently-structured real task (#7).

**Row #22 is a second, independent cross-substrate unification attempt (photonic vs. acoustic this time, not phononic/ternary/acoustic as in #20) and it also failed to unify — but via a different failure mode than #20.** Row #20's synthesis had two substrates that *were* measurable on the same terms (both had a real computation/robustness metric) and turned out to be mechanistically distinct once measured. Row #22's candidate synthesis fails one step earlier: the two source experiments were never designed with a comparable independent variable at comparable resolution, so the "do the curves share a shape" question isn't even answerable from existing data — the honest verdict is "not measurable as posed," not "measured and different." This is a distinct, worth-noting lesson: a surface-level cross-substrate rhyme can fail before mechanism is even reached, simply because the two datasets weren't built to be compared.

## Scoping entry — crystal-substrate candidate survey, 2026-08-21 (NOT a synthesis attempt)

Row #22 failed because the two datasets being compared (acoustic-vortex-trap steering density vs photonic spectral-channel spacing) were never built at comparable resolution — one had a real 8-point continuous sweep, the other only 2 discrete configurations. Rather than risk a third "not measurable as posed" failure by picking another substrate pair blind, this is a triage of four candidate *crystal*-based substrates for a possible future comparison, checking what real data already exists in this account versus what would need to be built from scratch. **This entry does not attempt or claim any synthesis; it is a scoping/triage note only, and is explicitly not a row in the results ledger above.**

1. **Phononic crystals (this project's own SSH-reservoir and Fibonacci/quasicrystal-connectivity work) — READY for a real comparison attempt today, no new data needed.** Two real, existing continuous sweeps already exist at resolution comparable to (or exceeding) the photonic spectral-hole sweep:
   - `phase_transition_defect.py`: a real 13-point continuous sweep of dimerization strength g in [-0.6, 0.6] against the FULL-state frozen-readout defect penalty — a genuine phase-transition-shaped curve (trivial vs. topological phase, boundary at g=0) with quantified behavior approaching the boundary.
   - `fibonacci_prefix_sweep.py` / `fibonacci_narma_scaling.py`: real 15-point and 13-point continuous N-scaling sweeps (N = 50 to 6500/10000, log-spaced) against a continuous NMSE cost-ratio outcome, verified prefix-nested (construction-artifact-free).
   Both are structurally the same test shape as the photonic sweep — a continuous physical/structural parameter against a continuous outcome, at 13-15 points vs. photonic's 8 — including, in the `phase_transition_defect.py` case, a sharp-transition region with quantifiable near-boundary behavior analogous to photonic's near-threshold fragility (0.9x spacing -> 1.85% error). This is the one candidate where a real comparison could be attempted right now without collecting new data. Note: readiness of the data is not a preview of the verdict — any actual attempt still needs its own confound hunt (e.g., checking whether "distance from phase boundary" and "channel spacing relative to linewidth" are truly playing the same functional role, not just superficially similar axis labels) before any claim is made.

2. **NV centers in diamond (optically-addressed spin defects, quantum memory/sensing) — NOT in this account; scope from scratch.** A targeted search across the account's project folders found zero substantive references (only unrelated file-cache noise matched the search terms). This is a real, well-established platform and a legitimate future candidate, but no existing quantitative work here touches it — a from-scratch study (defining what "defect-tolerance" or "structure vs. randomness" would even mean for spin-coherence readout) would be needed before any comparison, current or future.

3. **Ferroelectric/piezoelectric crystal memory (polarization-switching, the FeRAM mechanism) — NOT in this account; scope from scratch.** Same result as NV centers: no substantive existing data. Worth flagging as mechanistically the most genuinely distinct candidate of the four (polarization-switching bistability, not defect/topology-protected transport or spectral multiplexing) — which is a point in its favor for future work, since a real synthesis test needs mechanistically different substrates to be worth attempting at all, but it means this one requires the most new work before it's even measurable.

4. **Quasicrystals — not actually a fourth, independent substrate; already folded into the two substrates already compared in row #22.** Quasicrystal/aperiodic geometry already appears repeatedly inside the acoustic side (#8, #9: levitator quasicrystal element placement, only 2-3 discrete configurations, NULL then RETRACTED; #19: acoustic MEMS quasicrystal-perforated plate, NULL except one narrow symmetry-selection exception) and the phononic side (#13-15: Fibonacci/de Bruijn connectivity, the same continuous sweeps cited in candidate 1 above). There is no quasicrystal result in this account that stands outside the acoustic or phononic substrate it was measured in. Treated as a candidate bridge between acoustic and photonic, it isn't one — it's internal to the acoustic and phononic rows already on record, not a fifth or independent physical mechanism.

**Triage ranking (closest-to-measurable-today first):**
1. Phononic crystals (SSH defect-boundary sweep + Fibonacci/de Bruijn connectivity scaling) — real, comparable-resolution data exists now.
2. NV centers in diamond and ferroelectric/piezoelectric memory (tied) — real, well-established platforms, but zero existing account data; both need a from-scratch scoping study before any comparison is possible. Ferroelectric is the more mechanistically distinct of the two, which matters for whether a future synthesis attempt would even be worth running.
3. Quasicrystals — not a distinct candidate at all; already fully embedded in the acoustic (#8, #9, #19) and phononic (#13-15) rows already in this ledger.

## Row #24 — theoretical account attempt for #23's curve shapes, 2026-08-21 (PARTIAL — and a flagged repeat of a previously-retracted pattern)

**Flag before reading this row's verdict:** this task was framed as deriving a general rule — "topological/critical-point structure predicts a non-monotonic curve; its absence predicts a monotonic one" — intended to be applied predictively to *other* substrates (e.g., alternative NV-center-like hosts) before their curves are even measured. That is the same shape of claim as row #20 ("one law governs phononic/ternary/acoustic"), which was tried and retracted in this exact repo a month prior. Per this project's standing discipline, a claim of that shape should be flagged to the user as a repeat pattern before being treated as established, not just logged quietly as a positive result. It is flagged here explicitly; the verdict below is graded accordingly as partial, not confirmed.

**Item 1 — photonic monotonicity: rigorously derived, not just described.** `simulate_hole_burning.py`'s real crosstalk term is a rectangular-interval overlap fraction between two fixed-width (W = laser linewidth) windows spaced d apart: `overlap_frac(d) = max(0, 1 - d/W)`. This is provably monotonically non-increasing in d (slope is exactly -1/W below d=W, exactly 0 above it — never positive at any d), and clips to a hard zero at d=W (spacing = 1x linewidth), not an asymptote — which is exactly why the table shows a flat floor from 1x to 10x rather than continued gradual improvement. Given BER is an increasing function of this crosstalk term, monotonicity of BER-vs-spacing follows directly from the model's construction. This part is a genuine first-principles derivation, confirmed against the actual code, not a redescription of the shape.

**Item 2 — phononic shape: grounded in real physics, but incomplete.** Standard SSH theory (cited, not this repo's own prior derivation) says the bulk gap scales as Δ ~ 2|v-w| = 4|g| and, in the topological phase, the edge-state penetration depth scales as ξ ~ 1/ln(w/v), which diverges as g→0⁺ (the critical point) — real bulk-boundary-correspondence physics, and it does explain *some* features: why g=0 is special (gap closes, chiral-protected edge-state count flips 0→2), and why the topological and trivial sides are physically different regimes (different mode structure, not mirror images). It does **not**, on its own, derive the specific shape actually measured: a minimum at g=0, a rise to an asymmetric intermediate hump (trivial peak 1.427 at g=-0.5 vs. topological peak 0.544 at g=+0.3, 2.6x apart), and partial recovery by g=±0.6. A plausible alternative, non-topological explanation for the hump was considered — intermediate coupling strength generically producing the richest/most-distributed reservoir dynamics, hence the largest gap between a frozen and a retrained readout, independent of any topological invariant — and **no control exists in this repo to rule it out**: no script here sweeps a comparable coupling parameter in a chain with no discrete topological invariant (chiral symmetry deliberately broken, or no dimerization at all) over the same range to check whether a similar hump appears anyway. That control was not run for this task (would require new simulation, which was out of scope per the coordinator's "use only data you already have" framing) — so the topological explanation for the specific hump/asymmetry/recovery shape is plausible but **not confirmed to be topology-specific**, and this gap is reported explicitly rather than papered over.

**Item 3 — predictive claim: stated, but NOT validated, and flagged as a repeat of a previously-retracted claim shape.** The framework as requested would predict: substrates with topological/critical-point structure → non-monotonic, asymmetric curves; substrates without it (simple two-level spectral/spin-decoherence systems, e.g. diamond/SiC/silicon-based color centers as currently understood) → monotonic, saturating curves. Item 1 is solid; item 2 is real-but-incomplete; the specific hump/asymmetry/recovery mechanism was not shown to be uniquely topological. **Verdict: PARTIAL.** The framework correctly explains the photonic side from first principles and correctly identifies that *something* topology-specific happens near g=0 on the phononic side, but it does not rigorously derive the full phononic shape, has an untested confound (generic intermediate-coupling richness could produce a similar-looking hump without any topology at all), and its predictive extension to new substrates repeats, in a new guise, a cross-substrate unification pattern already tried and retracted once in this repo (#20). It should be treated as a testable hypothesis for the parallel NV-center-host survey to check against — not as an established law to interpret that survey's results through.

## Row #25 — cross-check of row #24's framework against real alternative-host literature, 2026-08-21 (row #24 DOWNGRADED: PARTIAL -> LEANS NULL)

Source: `crystal_substrate_alternative_hosts.md` (parallel literature-scoping pass, real citations, read directly before writing this row — not summarized secondhand). Findings: SiC divacancy (T₁⁻¹ = βC², monotonic quadratic), hBN V_B⁻ ensembles (T₂ ∝ ρ⁻⁰·⁸, monotonic power-law), and diamond NV (prior scoping, honest gap) all show **no topological connection** and, where a curve exists, it is **monotonic**. A distinct third shape — hBN donor-acceptor coupled pairs (Li, Pershin & Gali 2024/2025), threshold/critical-window at ~2 nm, converging asymptotically above it — was also found.

**Question 1 — does "no topology -> monotonic" across SiC/hBN/diamond count as real support for row #24, or is it confounded?** Confounded, and the evidence actually leans toward the null explanation, not a neutral tie. Every non-topological system checked (SiC, hBN ensemble, diamond) is *also* a simple pairwise/ensemble dipolar-bath decoherence model — the two candidate explanations ("no topology -> monotonic" vs. "simple decoherence models are monotonic by construction, topology was never the operative variable") are perfectly collinear across all three cases; no example exists of a non-topological but structurally complex system to break the tie, and none of the three papers proposes a topological mechanism at all (the survey's own search for one came back clean-negative). Occam's razor favors the simpler, topology-free explanation, since it fully accounts for all three curves without needing to invoke topological physics anywhere.

**Sharper problem, found on re-examination of this project's own row #23/#24 reference data, not the new literature:** the phononic penalty-vs-g curve is *already* non-monotonic **inside the purely trivial (non-topological, zero edge modes) region alone** — g=-0.6 -> penalty 1.329, g=-0.5 -> 1.427 (a local rise), then falling to 0.013 at g=0. A hump exists on the side of the boundary that has no protected edge-mode structure at all. This is direct internal evidence, not just an absent control, against "topology causes the non-monotonicity" — the effect survives even where topology's supposed mechanism (edge modes) is entirely absent, so something else (most plausibly the generic intermediate-coupling-richness alternative already flagged in row #24 as untested) is the more likely driver.

**Verdict: row #24 downgraded from PARTIAL to LEANS NULL.** The predictive claim ("topological structure -> non-monotonic; its absence -> monotonic") is not supported by the new cross-host evidence (confounded, Occam-disfavored) and is now directly undercut by a re-read of this project's own phononic reference curve (hump present in the topology-free half of the very same sweep). The framework's item 1 (photonic monotonicity, derived from the rectangular-overlap crosstalk model) still stands on its own rigorous terms; item 2/3 (topology as the source of the phononic hump, and its generalization to new substrates) should now be treated as not supported, pending an actual non-topological tunable-coupling control simulation that still does not exist in this repo.

**Question 2 — is the hBN donor-acceptor threshold/critical-window shape a genuine third taxonomy category?** The shape itself is real and distinct from both the photonic (monotonic-saturating) and phononic (interior hump) curves: unusable below ~2 nm, then asymptotic convergence above it, driven by ordinary Coulomb (1/R) and tunneling-overlap (exponential) terms — worth naming as a candidate third category. But it is not a clean fourth data point on the same terms as the other three: it describes a **pair** of two chemically distinct, coupled defects (donor-acceptor), not an **array** of many identical, individually-addressed channels the way the photonic channel array, phononic chain, and SiC/hBN ensembles all are. Comparing a pair-coupling curve to array/ensemble curves repeats exactly the unmatched-observable-class problem that sank row #22 — flagged here rather than forced. Logged as a real, distinct, but not-yet-comparable candidate shape, not a confirmed fourth member of the taxonomy.

## Discussion

**Why report this.** A single confirmed result and nineteen non-confirmations is not, on its face, an exciting headline — but the *pattern of failure* is informative in a way the individual negatives are not. The hypothesis "exotic/reinforcing structure improves computational robustness" was not tested once and abandoned; it was tested against topological lattices, magnetic coupling, quasi-periodic tilings, and a domain-specific engineered network, using genuinely different confound classes each time (input-locality, degree-matching, chiral symmetry, hop-distance) — and it lost or came back conditional nearly every time regardless of which specific confound was the threat. That is a real, if modest, calibration fact for anyone (including this researcher, going forward) proposing the next instance of this hypothesis shape: the prior should start skeptical, not favorable, and the burden of proof is a matched, confound-controlled baseline, not a raw comparison against an unmatched naive network.

**The one real exception is narrower than it first sounds.** Chiral-symmetry-conditional SSH defect-tolerance is the sole hypothesis in this ledger confirmed on more than one substrate (#1, #11) — but every attempt to broaden its scope failed: cross-architecture (#3, #4), full device physics without deliberate symmetry engineering (#5), a differently-structured real task (#7), and even the narrower "which disorder type" sub-prediction on the exact same LIF-chain substrate that confirmed the headline claim (#12). The lesson generalizes past this one mechanism: a structural effect confirmed under one specific set of conditions (task, substrate, disorder type, architecture comparison) should be assumed *not* to generalize to a change in any of those conditions until separately tested — confirmed in this ledger at least four separate times (#3 vs #1, #5 vs #1, #7 vs #1/#11, #12 vs #11).

**A recurring, specific failure mode: path/chain topologies are fragile by construction, not by disorder.** Rows #3, #4, and #21 independently converge on the same mechanism from three unrelated substrates: a linear-chain or feedback-dependent topology loses robustness not because structure fails to protect against *external* perturbation, but because the chain's own connectivity creates cut-vertices or single-point dependencies that a denser or simpler alternative doesn't have. In #21 specifically, the failure mode is new to this ledger: it is not that feedback fails to protect against unrelated disorder (the naive hypothesis), but that *depending on* feedback creates a longer, indirect dependency chain (the whole downstream cascade must fire once to arm the reinforcement) that a non-reinforced neuron with only two local synapses does not have. Denser/more-interdependent connectivity raising fragility, independently confirmed via three unrelated mechanisms (SSH path cut-vertices, RKKY/antiferro dense all-to-all coupling, and now jet-engine indirect feedback dependency), is arguably the most load-bearing sub-finding in this entire ledger.

**What would change this prior.** A hypothesis of this shape surviving (a) a matched/degree-controlled baseline, (b) more than one task type, (c) more than one disorder/perturbation class, and (d) a real (not idealized) physical or device-level substrate, simultaneously, would be a genuine break from the pattern above and worth treating as a real design primitive rather than a conditional curiosity. Nothing in this ledger has cleared all four simultaneously; #1/#2 clear (b) and part of (c); nothing clears (d) at full magnitude.

## Limitations

This is a synthesis of simulation studies (with one real-audio exception, #18, and one real-machine-telemetry exception, #21's driving data, though #21's network itself is simulated). The twenty-two rows are not a random or exhaustive sample of possible structure-vs-robustness hypotheses — they are the hypotheses this specific research program happened to test, largely because they were physically or mechanistically motivated at the time. A file-drawer effect in the *positive* direction is unlikely (every test here, positive or negative, is already on the public record), but the ledger cannot speak to hypotheses in this shape that were never attempted.
